## P1 — Ana, individual host (primary)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | 38, owns two apartments in a beach town (Costa Rica or Mexico). Works a day job and hosts on the side. |
| **Platforms** | Airbnb (main), Booking.com (one unit). No PMS. Uses the Airbnb app and scheduled messages. |
| **Guests** | 60% international (US, Canada, Europe), 40% domestic. |
| **Jobs to be done** | Answer guest questions fast without being glued to her phone. Give guests a smooth check-in. Earn a bit more per stay. |
| **Pains** | The same questions at 11 pm ("Where is the key box?", "Wi-Fi password?"). Answering in English takes her longer. Her PDF house manual goes unread. She would offer airport transfers but doesn't want to chase payments. |
| **Gains** | Five-star reviews; fewer messages; extra income with no extra work. |
| **Current tools** | A Google Doc house manual, Airbnb quick replies, a WhatsApp contact for a taxi driver. |
| **Decision factors** | Free or very cheap; set up in under 30 minutes from her phone; works in Spanish; no risk to her Airbnb account. |
| **Success looks like** | "Guests stopped asking me for the Wi-Fi password, and two of them booked the transfer through the guide." |

**Implications:**

- Guidesk must work without a PMS.
- Onboarding must be AI-assisted and mobile-friendly.
- The copilot covers Airbnb chat until an integration exists.
- The Free plan matters.

## P2 — Marco, professional property manager (primary)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | 45, runs a management company with 60 units across two US Sun Belt markets and one LATAM destination. Team of 3 guest-support agents. |
| **Platforms** | Airbnb, Vrbo, Booking.com, and direct bookings, all through Hostaway (or Guesty or Hospitable). |
| **Jobs to be done** | Cut inbox volume per unit; keep response times under 5 minutes at any hour; raise revenue per stay; keep owners happy. |
| **Pains** | Support headcount grows with units. Night shifts. Inconsistent answers between agents. Upsells handled ad hoc. Previous tools needed custom quotes and long onboarding. |
| **Gains** | Fewer agent hours per unit; measurable upsell revenue; consistent answers; reports he can show owners. |
| **Current tools** | His PMS's unified inbox and templates. Tried an AI auto-responder and turned it off after a wrong answer about parking. |
| **Decision factors** | Native integration with his PMS; control over what the AI can do; audit trail; ROI in the first month; team roles. |
| **Success looks like** | "70% of routine questions are handled automatically and safely, my team only sees what matters, and add-ons pay for the tool." |

**Implications:**

- PMS integrations (Phase 2) are a must for this segment.
- Configurable autonomy and escalation are non-negotiable.
- He needs analytics on time saved and revenue.

## P3 — Emily, guest (primary end user)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | 32, US traveler on a week-long trip with friends, staying in an Airbnb in Costa Rica. Doesn't speak Spanish. |
| **Context** | Arrives at night after a long drive. Mostly on mobile data, sometimes with poor coverage. |
| **Jobs to be done** | Get in without trouble; know how things work; find good local things to do; solve problems fast. |
| **Pains** | Hunting through long messages for the door code; waiting for the host to answer; generic tour suggestions. |
| **Gains** | Instant, correct answers; confidence that someone will help in an emergency; easy local recommendations. |
| **Expectations** | No app download, no account. Works in English. Doesn't feel like being sold to. |
| **Success looks like** | "I asked about the AC remote at midnight and got the answer in seconds. We booked a waterfall tour from the guide." |

**Implications:**

- The guide must be mobile-first, light enough for slow connections, and need no login.
- Sensitive information must be easy to reach for verified guests and invisible to everyone else.
- Offers must be contextual and disclosed, never pushy.

## P4 — Luis, local service provider (secondary, Phase 3)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | Runs a small transfer and tour business in a beach destination. |
| **Jobs to be done** | Get bookings from vacation-rental guests without paying high marketplace commissions. |
| **Pains** | Depends on hosts recommending him by word of mouth. Big tour marketplaces barely cover his destination. |
| **Success looks like** | "Hosts in my area list my transfers in their Guidesk guide and I get paid reliably." |

**Implications:** a provider-onboarding marketplace (Phase 3). It also strengthens Guidesk's local
inventory where the global affiliate catalogs are thin.

## Anti-personas (not targeted now)

- **Hotels and large hospitality chains.** Different operations and a crowded market (Duve,
  Operto).
- **Hosts who want Guidesk to manage pricing or calendars.** Guidesk is not a PMS.
